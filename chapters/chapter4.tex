\section{Thicknesses and refractive indices of a-Si and SiO\texorpdfstring{$_{2}$}{2} thin films}\label{sec: results_growth}
Figures~\ref{fig: thz_substance} and~\ref{fig: thz_subs} show the XRR measurements of various a-Si films grown on glass substrates with varying thicknesses according to the quartz crystal microbalance (QCM) readings. The measured thicknesses of the a-Si film based on XRR are summarized in Table~\ref{tab: thickness_a-Si}, while for SiO$_2$ the results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab: thickness_SiO2}. Linear regression of the measured thicknesses versus the QCM readings for a-Si as shown in Figure~\ref{fig: thz_subs} yields the equation $y = 167.82x + 2.749$ with $R^2 = 0.9999$, where $y$ is the measured XRR thickness in nanometers and $x$ is the QCM reading in kiloangstroms. Similarly, linear regression of the measured XRR thickness and the QCM values for SiO$_2$ as shown in Figure~\ref{fig: thz_subs} yields the equation $y = 199.49x - 5.4845$ with $R^2 = 0.9937$.

The results of the regression analysis indicate that the QCM readings during deposition scale linearly with the actual thicknesses of the films as measured by XRR. The high $R^2$ values suggest that the QCM is a reliable tool for monitoring film thickness during deposition, and the linear relationships can be used to calibrate the QCM readings for future depositions. The lower $R^2$ value in the SiO$_2$ regression is attributed to the variation in the deposition rate during the growth of the SiO$_2$ films, which is caused by the difficulty in maintaining a stable deposition rate for SiO$_2$ due to its low thermal conductivity and tendency to sublime instead of melting into a uniform liquid pool during heating by the electron beam as is the case for other materials. Figures~\ref{fig: thz_subs} and~\ref{fig: thz_subs} show the source materials inside the crucibles for electron beam evaporation, with the a-Si source material being a uniform melt and the SiO$_2$ source material showing localized heating and uneven melting.
