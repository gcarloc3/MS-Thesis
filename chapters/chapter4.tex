Linear regression of the measured thicknesses versus the \gls{QCM} readings for a-Si as shown in~\ref{fig: linear_fit_xrr_aSi} yields the equation 

\begin{equation} \label{eqn:aSi_thickness} 
    y = 168.03x + 3.0403
\end{equation}

with $R^2 = 0.9999$, where $y$ is the measured XRR thickness in \unit{\nano\meter} and $x$ is the \gls{QCM} reading in \unit{\kilo\angstrom}. Similarly, linear regression of the measured XRR thickness and the \gls{QCM} values for SiO$_2$ as shown in~\ref{fig: linear_fit_xrr_SiO2} yields the following equation with $R^2 = 0.9941$:

\begin{equation} \label{eqn:SiO2_thickness} 
    y = 198.52x - 4.5056
\end{equation}

The initial comparison of the \gls{QCM} readings and the measured thicknesses during XRR indicate that the \gls{QCM} readings during deposition generally underestimate the actual thicknesses of the films. This underestimation is likely due to the mismatch in densities used as input for the \gls{QCM} as shown in~\ref{tab: qcm_input} and the actual densities of the evaporated film as shown in~\ref{tab: XRR_fit}. The thicknesses of the films as obtained by curve fitting generally agree with the direct and Fourier method of analysis for the Kiessig fringes. The low interface roughness of the films imply that electron beam evaporation is an effective method to produce uniform films, albeit with much lower densities than the bulk material. This discrepancy may be caused by the increased porosity of the film. The lack of data for films thicker than \qty{1.52}{\kilo\angstrom} for a-Si and \qty{1.369}{\kilo\angstrom} for SiO$_2$ is explained by the limitation of the equipment, which can only resolve fringes larger than that which correspond to a thickness of \qty{270}{\nano\meter}.

Nevertheless, the results of the regression analysis indicate that the \gls{QCM} readings scale linearly with the actual thicknesses of the films as measured by XRR\@. The high $R^2$ values suggest that the \gls{QCM} is a reliable tool for monitoring film thickness during deposition as long as the linear relationship is considered, and that the linear relationships obtained in this study can be used to calibrate the \gls{QCM} readings for future depositions. The lower $R^2$ value in the regression for SiO$_2$ is attributed to the variation in the deposition rate during the growth of the SiO$_2$ films, which is caused by the difficulty in maintaining a stable deposition rate for SiO$_2$ due to its low thermal conductivity and tendency to sublime instead of melting into a uniform liquid pool during heating by the electron beam as is the case for other materials.

\section{Refractive index of a-Si thin films}\label{sec: results_index_Si}
~\ref{fig: swanelpoel_aSi} shows the UV-Vis transmittance spectra of a \qty{600}{\nano\meter} a-Si thin film deposited on a glass substrate together with the upper and lower envelopes as calculated using the envelope method as described in~\ref{sec: swanepoel}. The equations of the exponential functions fitted to the extrema points listed in~\ref{tab: swanepoel_aSi} are
\begin{equation} \label{eqn:aSi_upper_env} 
    y = 80.612419+\num{1.200883e9}e^{-x/89.819343}-\num{12.00749e9}e^{-x/89.821415}
\end{equation}
for the upper envelope with $R^2 = 0.9969$ and 
\begin{equation} \label{eqn:aSi_lower_env} 
    y = 45.9178758+\num{3.6028012e10}e^{-x/64.3969697}-\num{3.16015549e10}e^{-x/64.3973202}
\end{equation}
for the lower envelope with $R^2 = 0.9977$. The last $d_{1}$ of \qty{281.5}{\nano\meter} is excluded from further calculations due to its distance from the other thickness values. The equation for the refractive index of the a-Si thin film according to \ref{eqn: sellmeier} fitted to the data points of the refractive indices obtained from the envelope method is
\begin{equation} \label{eqn:aSi_sellmeier} 
    n^2 = 14.84529+\frac{\num{7.06709e12}\lambda^2}{\lambda^2+\num{2.12449e18}}+\frac{-10.41841\lambda^2}{\lambda^2+190469.027}.
\end{equation}
and the graph of the resulting refractive index versus wavelength is shown in~\ref{fig: index_aSi}. The average thickness of the a-Si thin film according to the envelope method is \qty{653}{\nano\meter}.

The refractive index of the a-Si thin film is comparable to the literature values obtained by Swanepoel for a-Si, with values ranging from $3.0$ to $3.2$ across the relevant wavelengths. However, the refractive index does not decrease monotonically with increasing wavelength as expected for the material. This deviation from the expected behavior is unlikely to be anomalous dispersion and instead is probably caused by the few number of data points from the envelope method used to fit the Sellmeier equation. This is supported by the difference in calculated thickness for the film using the envelope method and the extrapolation using~\ref{eqn:aSi_thickness}. A larger number of oscillations and data points would likely yield a more accurate Sellmeier equation that would exhibit the proper dispersion behavior across the wavelength range\cite{heavensOpticalPropertiesThin1960}. Furthermore, the a-Si thin film refractive index is much lower than that of crystalline silicon, which has a refractive index of $3.5$ to $4.25$ across the range of wavelengths as measured by Schinke\cite{fluggeHandbuchPhysikRontgenstrahlen1957}. This is likely due to the lower density and less ordered structure of the a-Si thin film compared to crystalline silicon, and supports the previous finding in~\ref{sec: results_thicknesses}.

The refractive index of the SiO$_2$ thin film is comparable to the literature values obtained from Gao\cite{liuOptimalGrowthConditions2022}. The slight increase in refractive index versus the literature values could be attributed to the stoichiometry of the SiO$_2$ thin film caused by a lack of reactive gas during the deposition process, leading to a higher refractive index closer to that of silicon. Miyazaki et al.\ deposited SiO$_{1.74}$ thin films and found that the refractive index at \qty{633}{\nano\meter} was $1.50$, which is higher than the measured refractive index in this work ($1.49$). The decreased ratio of oxygen versus silicon may be another reason for the lower density of the SiO$_2$ thin film as measured by XRR previously compared to bulk SiO$_2$ aside from the increased porosity of the film.

The multilayer coatings deposited using electron beam deposition have reduced values of reflectance compared to the bare wafer, proving their effectiveness as antireflective coatings. The mismatch in the minimum reflectance values obtained experimentally versus the simulation is due to the nature of the experimental setup, which measures the reflectance of the sample relative to the mirror. More accurate reflectance values would be obtained from absolute measurements which consider the total amount of light incident and reflected from the sample. Interestingly, the deviation of wavelengths between the measured minima of the films and that of their simulated counter parts increases as the target wavelength increases; a difference of \qty{7}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{656}{\nano\meter}, which increases to \qty{34}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{684}{\nano\meter}, and finally \qty{62}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{656}{\nano\meter}. This shift in values is likely caused by the inaccuracy in measuring the refractive index of the thin films which were previously deposited, especially for a-Si. The refractive index values calculated may have been accurate at the lower wavelengths but increasingly erroneous for longer wavelengths, which corroborates the observation in~\ref{sec: results_index_Si} where the index does not monotonically decrease with increasing wavelength.
