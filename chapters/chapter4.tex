The Kiessig fringes discussed in~\ref{sec: xrr} are apparent from~\ref{fig: a-Si_XRR} and their size varies depending on the QCM thickness. The largest fringes appear in the \qty{0.035}{\kilo\angstrom} thick film and get progressively smaller as the \gls{QCM} thickness reading increases, with the \qty{1.520}{\kilo\angstrom} film having very small fringes. No fringes could be identified for films beyond this thickness, which is attributed to the limited resolution of the x-ray diffractometer. The step size of the machine is , which implies that any fringes smaller than this can no longer be resolved. In~\ref{tab: thickness_a-Si} the results of the direct and Fourier method are in general agreement, signifying the accuracy of the \gls{XRR} method in determining the thickness of the film.

Linear regression of the \gls{XRR} and \gls{QCM} thicknesses as shown in~\ref{fig: linear_fit_xrr_aSi} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:aSi_thickness} 
    y = 168.03x + 3.0403
\end{equation}
with $R^2 = 0.9999$, where $y$ is the measured XRR thickness in \unit{\nano\meter} and $x$ is the \gls{QCM} reading in \unit{\kilo\angstrom}. The high $R^2$ value signifies that this equation relating the \gls{QCM} thickness to the \gls{XRR} thickness is reliable and can be used to match the real-time \gls{QCM} readings during deposition to the actual thickness of the deposited film.

\section{Refractive index of a-Si thin films}\label{sec: results_index_Si}

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/swanelpoel_aSi.png}
	\caption[Envelope method for \qty{600}{\nano\meter} a-Si thin film]{The transmission curves for the \qty{600}{\nano\meter} a-Si thin film $T_{a-Si}$ and the glass substrate $T_{sub}$ together with the upper $T_{M}$ and lower envelopes $T_{m}$ for the envelope method. The envelopes cross at $\lambda=$ \qty{630}{\nano\meter}, likely caused by the very few data points used for fitting as highlighted.}\label{fig: swanelpoel_aSi}
\end{figure}

~\ref{fig: swanelpoel_aSi} shows the UV-Vis transmittance spectra of a \qty{600}{\nano\meter} a-Si thin film deposited on a glass substrate together with the upper and lower envelopes as calculated using a modified version of the envelope method described in~\ref{sec: swanepoel}. The equations of the exponential functions fitted to the extrema points listed in~\ref{tab: swanepoel_aSi} using \verb|scipy.optimize.curve_fit| are
\begin{align}
	y &= 0.7848 - 250.7271e^{-x/103.5957} \label{eqn:aSi_env_1} \\
	y &= 0.4543 - 464.5691e^{-x/83.2507} \label{eqn:aSi_env_2}
\end{align}
for the upper and lower envelopes respectively. The last maxima at $\lambda=$ \qty{655}{\nano\meter} is excluded from further calculations due to the mismatch in expected value for $m$. The envelope method in~\ref{sec: swanepoel} specifies that succeeding extrema must correspond to half-integer steps, but the calculated value at this wavelength is $m=5.0$ instead of a value close to the half-integer $5.5$. This likely means that the envelope functions are no longer accurate at this wavelength. The minima at $\lambda=$ \qty{613}{\nano\meter} is also excluded because the calculated $N$ is negative, caused by the crossing of the upper and lower envelopes. More fringes in the transmission spectra would probably result in more accurate envelopes which satisfy the necessary conditions outlined in~\ref{sec: swanepoel}.

The equation for the refractive index of the a-Si thin film according to the Cauchy ~\ref{eqn: sellmeier} fitted to the remaining four data points of the refractive indices obtained from the envelope method using \verb|scipy.optimize.curve_fit| is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:aSi_sellmeier} 
    n(\lambda) = 2.8716 + \frac{38061.0}{\lambda^2}
\end{equation}
with $R^2 \approx 0.5849$ and the graph of the resulting refractive index versus wavelength is shown in~\ref{fig: index_aSi}. The low $R^2$ is caused by the refractive index at \qty{871}{\nano\meter} deviating from the trend of increasing refractive index for decreasing wavelength, likely due to the transmission envelope.

On the other hand, direct curve fitting of the Cauchy equation to the transmission spectra using the transfer matrix method and \verb|scipy.optimize.curve_fit| as shown in~\ref{fig: T_fit_aSi}, gives the following equation for refractive index 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:aSi_direct_cauchy} 
    n(\lambda) = (3.2971\pm0.0128) + \frac{0\pm9886.9}{\lambda^2}
\end{equation}
as graphed in \ref{fig: index_aSi}. Values lower than \qty{700}{\nano\meter} had to be excluded from the fitting in order for the model to converge properly, which means that the refractive index values corresponding to wavlengths lower than \qty{700}{\nano\meter} are an extrapolation.

	\caption[Refractive index of a-Si thin film]{Refractive index of the a-Si thin film from \qtyrange{500}{1100}{\nano\meter} as calculated from the envelope and direct fitting methods compared to values obtained by Swanepoel\cite{swanepoelDeterminationThicknessOptical1983} and Schinke\cite{schinkeUncertaintyAnalysisCoefficient2015}.}\label{fig: index_aSi}
\end{figure}

The refractive indices of the a-Si thin film calculated for both methods are quite consistent, differing by around $0.25$, and comparable to the literature values obtained by Swanepoel for a-Si at longer wavelengths\cite{swanepoelDeterminationThicknessOptical1983}. Furthermore, the calculated a-Si thin film refractive index is lower than that of crystalline silicon, which has a refractive index of $3.5$ to $4.25$ across the range of wavelengths as measured by Schinke~\cite{schinkeUncertaintyAnalysisCoefficient2015}. This is likely due to the lower density and less ordered structure of the a-Si thin film compared to crystalline silicon, and supports the previous findings in~\ref{sec: results_thicknesses}. However, the refractive index appears somewhat flat over the wavelength range instead of increasing at shorter wavelengths. This difference from the expected behavior at shorter wavelengths is probably caused by the loss of information due to the fitting procedures used.

In the modified envelope method, the number of data points used to fit the transmission envelopes is too few, which results in less accurate transmission envelopes and consequently refractive index values. On the other hand, for the direct fitting method using the transfer matrix method, the accuracy of the calculated value depends on the quality of the fit to the experimental data and is also limited to the \qtyrange{700}{1100}{\nano\meter} wavelength range. An extrapolation to shorter wavelengths may no longer be accurate regardless of the method used.

The refractive index of the SiO$_2$ thin film is comparable to the literature values obtained from Gao\cite{liuOptimalGrowthConditions2022}. The slight increase in refractive index versus the literature values could be attributed to the stoichiometry of the SiO$_2$ thin film caused by a lack of reactive gas during the deposition process, leading to a higher refractive index closer to that of silicon. Miyazaki et al.\ deposited SiO$_{1.74}$ thin films and found that the refractive index at \qty{633}{\nano\meter} was $1.50$~\cite{miyazakiRefractiveIndexDielectric2010}, which is higher than the measured refractive index in this work ($1.49$). The decreased ratio of oxygen versus silicon may be another reason for the lower density of the SiO$_2$ thin film as measured by XRR previously compared to bulk SiO$_2$ aside from the increased porosity of the film.

The multilayer coatings deposited using electron beam deposition have reduced values of reflectance compared to the bare wafer, proving their effectiveness as antireflective coatings. The mismatch in the minimum reflectance values obtained experimentally versus the simulation is due to the nature of the experimental setup, which measures the reflectance of the sample relative to the mirror. More accurate reflectance values would be obtained from absolute measurements which consider the total amount of light incident and reflected from the sample. Interestingly, the deviation of wavelengths between the measured minima of the films and that of their simulated counter parts increases as the target wavelength increases; a difference of \qty{7}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{656}{\nano\meter}, which increases to \qty{34}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{684}{\nano\meter}, and finally \qty{62}{\nano\meter} at a target wavelength of \qty{656}{\nano\meter}. This shift in values is likely caused by the inaccuracy in measuring the refractive index of the thin films which were previously deposited, especially for a-Si. The refractive index values calculated may have been accurate at the lower wavelengths but increasingly erroneous for longer wavelengths, which corroborates the observation in~\ref{sec: results_index_Si} where the index does not monotonically decrease with increasing wavelength.
